American cockroach

Exterior perimeter: species and label check

Termidor SC: American cockroach is not individually named in the reviewed Termidor SC pest list. The label names Australian, Oriental and smokybrown cockroaches. This guide does not assign a verified Termidor match to this species by borrowing the Oriental listing. This is a label-match finding, not a complete interpretation of every legal unlisted-pest exception.

California exterior foundation use: maximum 6 inches up and 6 inches out; 0.03% dilution only; maximum 4 applications per calendar year, at least 60 days apart; no applications November 1–February 28. Do not treat garage doors, driveways, vertical surfaces above them, or the prohibited adjacent cracks/crevices. Refer to product label.

Other products: distinguish a general “cockroaches” listing from an individually named species. A general listing is not a species-specific efficacy claim. The outdoor site, target, application method, and California restrictions must be checked together; an indoor roach listing does not establish exterior perimeter permission.

Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

Winged reproductives (alates) can arise from multiple species already in EIQ, including carpenter, pavement, Argentine and odorous house ants. Queen and male winged forms vary by species; not every swarm can be identified to species by wings alone.

Kitchen small-fly identification: fruit flies vs. drain flies vs. phorid flies

Fruit flies: Often associated with fermenting produce, food residue and recycling. Drain flies: Fuzzy, moth-like adults; inspect organic film in drains. Phorid flies: Humpbacked profile and rapid running; inspect wet decaying organic material and hidden plumbing leaks.

IPM: inspect breeding sites, remove residue, scrub drains where appropriate, eliminate moisture sources and use monitoring traps. Apply pesticides only when the exact label allows the target pest, site and method.

German vs. brown-banded cockroach

German cockroach: two dark parallel lengthwise stripes on the pronotum behind the head; nymphs have a pale longitudinal stripe. Common in warm, moist kitchen and bathroom harborages.

Brown-banded cockroach: two pale cross-bands on wings or abdomen; often found in warmer, drier elevated furniture or rooms away from water. Compare adult and nymph patterns before assigning a species.

Drain vs. phorid vs. fruit fly

Drain fly: fuzzy, broad wings held roof-like; inspect wet drain film. Phorid fly: distinct humped thorax, short erratic runs; investigate decaying wet organic matter and possible plumbing leaks. Fruit fly: compact tan or brown body, frequently red eyes but not always; check fermenting produce, residue and recycling.

Confirmation step: inspect the actual breeding source; adult fly appearance alone does not establish the source.

Bed bug vs. carpet beetle larva

Bed bug nymph: six legs, flattened oval body, visible antennae, no wings and no dense coat of bristles. Five nymphal stages. Carpet beetle larva: elongated or tapered, distinctly hairy or bristly, may leave shed skins near lint, wool, or stored fabrics.

Do not treat an unidentified shed skin or fabric stain as proof of bed bugs.

Ant swarmers vs. termite swarmers — referral check

Winged ant: pinched waist, elbowed antennae, two wing pairs with front pair commonly larger. Winged termite: broad waist, straighter bead-like antennae and two pairs of roughly equal-length wings.

Scope: Termite identification is for recognizing and referring possible Branch III work; EntomoIQ remains a Branch II guide.

Silverfish vs. firebrat

Both have tapered wingless bodies, long antennae and three tail filaments. Silverfish: commonly silvery and associated with damp, cool spaces. Firebrat: mottled brown-gray and often encountered near warm equipment or heat sources. Temperature and color are clues, not proof by themselves.
